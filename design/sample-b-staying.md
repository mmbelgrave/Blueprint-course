# Sample B: change your life where you are (made up)

A worked example of Steps 1 to 3 for somebody who decides to stay. Sam is
invented, like Anna. Nothing here is a testimonial, and nothing here is a
promise about what anybody else will get.

Every heading and every question below is the app's own. The labels are copied
from `step1-content.json`, `step2-content.json` and `step3-content.json`, so
this reads the way the real result documents read. Only the answers are made
up.

**Sam**, early 40s. Lives in the town where Sam lives, works full time in an
office. Wants more time outdoors and fewer work evenings. Does not want to
move.

---

## After Step 1: My Working Direction

**A normal day in your new life** (page 1.2)

> I wake up without an alarm and the house is quiet. I work from nine until
> half past five, and then I stop. I walk by the river before dinner, in the
> light. On Friday I am outside all day, with my hands dirty and my phone in a
> drawer. I eat with people I know, in the town I already live in.

**What I want to keep** (page 1.4)

> My job and the people in it. My flat and the rent I can afford. My sister
> ten minutes away. The Thursday market. Knowing the chemist by name.

**My life picture in three sentences**

> I stay in the town I know, and I get my evenings back. I work four days, and
> the fifth day is outdoors. I am outside every day, even when the weather is
> bad.

**What I choose now**

> Build the life I want where I am

**The direction(s) I want to explore**

> A four-day week in the same job, and two evenings that end at six. Same
> town, same work, a different week.

**Why: two or three reasons**

> I do not want a new life. I want my own life, with the evenings in it.
> Everything I would miss is here already. Moving would cost me the people who
> make an ordinary week good.

**My essential needs: I take these with me**

> Daylight after work. One day a week outdoors. Rent I can pay without
> counting. My sister close enough to visit.

**Assumptions I still need to check**

> That my manager will discuss a four-day week at all. That I can live on
> four-fifths of my pay. That I will actually go outside when the evening is
> free, instead of staying in.

**What I need to learn next**

> What two free evenings a week really feel like after the first week.

**My next small step, within two weeks**

> No work after six on Tuesday and Thursday, for four weeks.

**When I will review what I learned**

> In 8 weeks.

**What result would make me continue, change direction, or stop**

> If I am outside on most of those evenings, I ask about the four-day week. If
> I work through them anyway, the problem is not my hours, and I look at that
> instead.

## After Step 2: what I tried where I live

Step 2 is called Explore. For somebody staying, the exploring happens at home:
small experiments, each one checked against the picture from Step 1.

**What I am looking for** (page s2-0.1, carried over from Step 1)

> Picture: evenings in daylight, one day a week outdoors, same town.
> Must-haves: daylight after work, one outdoor day, rent I can pay.
> Dealbreakers: a week with no daylight in it. Losing my sister nearby.

**The real-life checklist** (page s2-4.2), four experiments

| Place | What I felt | What I saw and measured | Score 1–10 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| No work after 18:00, Tuesday and Thursday, four weeks | Twitchy in the first week. Normal by the third. | Kept it 6 evenings out of 8. Went outside on 5 of them. Nobody at work noticed. | 8 |
| One Friday off, unpaid, to test the fifth day | Long and slow in a good way. | Garden in the morning, river in the afternoon. Cost me a day of pay. Did not look at email. | 9 |
| The allotment waiting list | Hopeful, and a bit silly for not asking sooner. | 14 months of waiting. Two people offered me a corner of theirs in the meantime. | 6 |
| Walking to work twice a week | Better than the gym I do not go to. | 35 minutes each way. Needs leaving at 8:05. Only works in dry weather. | 7 |

**The honest evening question** (page s2-4.3)

> **Could I live an ordinary week here, with laundry, work, bad weather and
> nothing special happening?** Yes. The ordinary week is the part I like. What
> I could not live with was the evening disappearing into work.
>
> **What did I notice that I would rather have overlooked?** Twice I kept the
> evening free and then filled it with work anyway, because nobody asked me
> not to.
>
> **What surprised me in a good way?** Nothing happened at work when I stopped
> at six. I had expected something to go wrong.

**If you decide to stay** (page s2-5.3)

> **What I now know I really want:** my evenings and one outdoor day. Not a
> different country.
>
> **What I will change in the next three months:** a four-day week, with
> Friday outdoors, and two evenings that end at six.
>
> **What staying protects, and what it costs me:** it protects my sister
> nearby, my rent, and work I am good at. It costs me a fifth of my pay, and
> the idea that something better is somewhere else.
>
> **What must be true before I look abroad again?** Nothing right now. I would
> look again if the work stopped or if the people I stay for moved away.
>
> **When I will look at this again:** in 8 weeks.

## After Step 3: My Decision, and My Change Plan

**My Decision** (page s3-4.2)

> **My decision question:** do I change my life where I am, or do I move?
>
> **My decision:** Go and change where I am
>
> **Must-haves met, and no dealbreakers broken?** Yes. Daylight after work and
> one outdoor day are both in the plan. Nothing I said I could not live with
> is in it.
>
> **Why: my three main reasons:** what I want is here. The experiment worked
> on six evenings out of eight. Moving would cost me the people I stay for.
>
> **My lights:** Money is amber until I have run four-fifths of my pay through
> a real month. People is green. Place is green. Papers does not apply.
> Plan B is green.
>
> **Conditions that must be true before the big steps:** one full month on
> four-fifths pay, with the rent and the bills paid from it.
>
> **Who decided with me, and do we agree?** My sister. Yes.
>
> **My first step:** ask my manager for a meeting about a four-day week.
>
> **Date of my first step:** in the first week after this decision.
>
> **When will I review this decision?** In 8 weeks.
>
> **What would make me change it?** A no from work with no room in it, or a
> month on the lower pay that does not add up.

**My Change Plan** (the Step 3 result for a staying route)

> **The change I am making:** four days a week in the same job, with Friday
> outdoors, and two evenings a week that end at six.
>
> **The date it is in place:** in the fourth month.
>
> **My first three steps, and when:** ask for the meeting, in the first week.
> Run one full month on four-fifths pay on paper, in the first month. Put the
> request in writing with my notice period, in the second month.
>
> **My income and buffer after the change:** four-fifths of my pay from the
> fourth month. The buffer stays at three months of rent and bills, untouched.
>
> **Who I tell, and what I need from them:** my manager first, then my team.
> From my sister, somebody to walk with on a Friday so I do not waste the day
> indoors.
>
> **My first 90 days:** week one, the meeting. After 30 days, two evenings a
> week that really end at six. After 90 days, the first Friday outdoors, and
> no work email on it.
>
> **My Plan B:** if the four-day week is refused, I keep the two evenings and
> ask again in six months. If my pay will not stretch, I take one unpaid day a
> month instead of a whole day a week.
>
> **My check-in dates:** three months after the change, and one year after it.

**Your change, planned backwards** (page s3-5.1)

| Step | What must be true first | How long it takes | I start by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ask for the four-day week | A month of figures I believe | one meeting | the first week |
| Live on four-fifths pay on paper | Last three months of spending in front of me | 2 weeks | the first month |
| Put the request in writing | A yes in principle | my notice period | the second month |
| First Friday outdoors | The change in place | 1 day | the fourth month |

**Hours of "my choice" per week now, and after the change** (page s3-5.3)

> Now: about 6. After: about 16.

---

The decision names what to change and what to check next. It is not only a yes
or no to moving.
