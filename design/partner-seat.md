# The partner seat, page by page

A proposal, 15 October 2026, after Mwata's point: a lot of this course is done
together, so two identical copies of the app would be wrong. Some pages want
one answer for the household. Others want two answers and then a comparison.

This file is the page-by-page decision. Mark it up and it becomes §6.8 of
PROJECT.md.

## Three kinds of page

**Ours.** One set of answers for the household. Either person can write, and
the page says who changed it last. All the money, all the facts about a place,
the timeline, the lights, the decision being tested. Two people cannot have
two different savings totals.

**Mine.** Each person answers alone. Nothing is shown to the other until both
have written, and then the page shows the two answers side by side. The wishes,
the scores, the feelings, the must-haves, the dealbreakers.

**Read.** Explanation, no answer. Nothing to share.

## Why "Mine" needs the waiting rule

If Ben can see Lotte's must-haves before he writes his own, he writes hers. The
value of the comparison is that neither saw the other's answer first. So a
"Mine" page shows "Lotte has written this one" and nothing else until you have
written too.

Each person can also keep one page private for good: 2.3 (what stopped you
before), 2.4 (beliefs) and 3.1 (what money means to you) are the ones people
may not want to hand over. Private is per page, set by the person who wrote it,
and can be turned off again. It never unlocks by itself.

Roughly thirty pages each way, and four to read. That is the answer to "does a
second copy of the app make sense": no, half of it is joint.

## What the result documents become

Most of what the four documents print comes from "Ours" pages, so a couple gets
**one** My Decision and **one** Blueprint, with both names on the cover. The
personal parts print twice, which is what the design proposals of 9 October
already did: they quoted Lotte and Ben separately. That was written off as
impossible because one account was one person. With the seat it is the natural
shape.

Where the two differ and it matters, the document says so in one line: a
must-have only one of them wrote down, a dealbreaker only one of them has, two
different answers to the honest evening question.

## The rules that keep it safe

1. Nothing from a "Mine" page reaches the other person until both have written.
2. A person can mark their own "Mine" page private, and it stays private.
3. Each person keeps their own AI partner, their own chats and their own notes.
   The AI sees your own answers and the shared answers, never the other
   person's private side, and it may only talk about a comparison on a page
   that is already open to you both.
4. The shared answers belong to the buyer's account. If the seat ends, the
   household answers stay with the buyer and the partner keeps nothing but
   their own.
5. One seat per purchase, set once. Changing it is an email to Mwata.

## How it would be built

Three slices, each worth having on its own.

**1. The seat, and shared answers.** A `partner_seats` table, the invite in
Settings, the sign-in authorisation, and the "Ours" pages genuinely shared:
written to the buyer's row, with the server deciding whose answers a page
reads. Every page gets a `together` field in the content JSON, from the tables
above. After this slice a couple has one money picture and one Decision, which
is the thing that is wrong today.

**2. The comparison.** The waiting rule, the private switch, and the side by
side view on "Mine" pages and in the part summaries. The agreed line on 5.1 and
the agreed decision on 4.2.

**3. Two voices in the documents.** Both names, both wheels, both must-have
lists, and the difference lines.

The parts to be careful with: the money rules, which are the most tested code
in the app and must keep counting "unknown" as unknown when two people fill in
one table; row-level security, because a household reading each other's rows is
a new kind of access and the current policies say "your own rows only"; and the
AI context, which must not carry the other person's answers.

## Two things to decide

**Is the seat free with any purchase?** The terms say the course may be used
with the people you plan your move with, and §6.8 says each purchase may invite
one partner, Phase 1 included. The cost is that one purchase can run two AI
partners. A pooled monthly AI allowance per household keeps that where it was
planned, rather than one allowance each.

**Does the classification above read right?** It is a course decision, not a
code decision. The ones worth arguing about: 4.1 (shared options, private
feelings), the green lights (one set, or one each), and 4.2 (I think both
should answer alone first, and that this is the most valuable thing the seat
can do).

## Three questions Mwata asked (15 October)

### Does the comparison get its own tab?

No, and for the reason the bar has four places and not five: five labels do not
fit a 375-pixel phone (his own decision, 6 October). A fifth tab would also put
the comparison somewhere other than the work, and the comparison is the work.

It appears in three places instead:

1. **On the page itself.** A "Mine" page shows your box and theirs once both
   have written, as in the mockup.
2. **In the part summary.** "What does this tell me?" already gathers a part.
   It gains the two panels: what you both wrote, and what only one of you
   wrote.
3. **One gathered view per step**, reached from the step overview beside the
   result, for the conversation you have at the end of a step. Not a tab: a
   card called "You and Ben".

If the seat gets used and that card turns out to be where people live, it can
be promoted later. The data does not change, only where it is read.

### What does the partner's app look like?

The same app. The partner is not a visitor with a cut-down copy.

- They see every page, and they write the shared ones. Often the partner is
  the one who does the research and the money table, so locking them out of it
  would break the course.
- "Mine" pages are their own to answer, and they see the comparison on the same
  terms the buyer does.
- They get their own AI partner, their own notes, their own chats.
- The difference is ownership, not access: the purchase and the shared answers
  live on the buyer's account, so the household answers stay with the buyer if
  the seat ever ends.

One consequence to build deliberately: progress. A shared page that is finished
is finished for both, so `exercise_status` for a shared page has to be the
household's status, not each person's. Otherwise the partner opens Step 1 and
sees a filled-in money table marked "not started".

### How fresh is the partner's input?

Today the app loads every answer once, when it starts, and saves each field
about a second after typing stops. There is no live connection. For the seat
that gives:

- **A shared page**: the partner's writing is there when the app is opened or
  reloaded, not keystroke by keystroke. Good enough for two people sitting at
  one table, and for the far more common case of one of them doing the research
  on Tuesday and the other reading it on Thursday.
- **The one real risk**, two people in the same money table at the same moment.
  Answers are stored per field with an `updated_at`, so adding `changed_by` is
  enough to catch it: on save, if that field changed since the page was loaded,
  the page says "Ben changed this 4 minutes ago" and offers keep or replace.
  Nothing is lost silently.
- **A waiting comparison**: while a "Mine" page is open and waiting for the
  other person, ask the server every twenty seconds or so. It is one small
  query, and it means the comparison appears by itself when they finish in the
  next room.
- **Opening a step**: one query asks what is new since you last looked, and a
  quiet line says "Ben has written 1.4 and the money table" with a refresh.

Supabase Realtime, a live subscription, is deliberately not in this plan. It
adds a connection per person and a new set of access questions, and the only
thing it buys over the twenty-second check is seeing a word appear as the other
person types it. If couples turn out to work side by side in two browsers, it
can be added to the shared pages alone.

### Nobody invited, nothing new on screen

A person working alone sees the app exactly as it is today. No "Ours" or "Mine"
labels, no comparison panels, no "You and Ben" card, no page waiting for
somebody else, and a single-voice result document. The only new thing is the
invitation card in Settings, which is also how the seat gets offered.

The classification sits in the content either way; with a household of one it
has nothing to do, and answers are stored on your own account as they are now.

**Inviting later costs nothing.** Shared pages live on the buyer's account, so
a buyer who has already filled in the money table simply has a household that
now has two people in it: nothing moves and nothing is rewritten. Their
personal pages become comparable as the partner writes each one, and the
waiting rule still does its job, because the partner has not seen the buyer's
answer and the buyer wrote theirs with nobody to copy. If the seat ever ends,
the extra screens disappear again and the shared answers stay with the buyer.
